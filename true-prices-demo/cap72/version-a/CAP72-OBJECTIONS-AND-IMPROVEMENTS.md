# CAP-72: why it stalled, and what would fix it

Written Oct 20, 2026 for the agent-wallet demo under `true-prices-demo/cap72/`. Everything in section 2 marked "verified" was read in the linked source. Anything marked "(inference)" is reasoning from the sources, not something anyone said. Quotes are exact unless marked "paraphrase".

Sources read: the CAP text and its git history in `stellar/stellar-protocol` ([cap-0072.md](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0072.md), commits [6afb0f9](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/commit/6afb0f93cc5d0564d39c0bb070881b995a751c7b) and [7699c72](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/commit/7699c72ce286e725b7bca40dc4079a269b19a792)), [CAP-71](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0071.md) and [CAP-71-01](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0071-01.md), [CAP-73](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0073.md), the full [discussion #1763](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763) (7 comments, 14 replies, now marked Closed), the [CAP-71 discussion #1784](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1784), [PR #1786](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/pull/1786) and [PR #1789](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/pull/1789), [nidohq/nido#165](https://github.com/nidohq/nido/issues/165), and the [2025-09-25 developer meeting page](https://developers.stellar.org/meetings/2025/09/25). The meeting page only links the three CAPs; its [recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bTiFz8VHQyQ) has no fetchable transcript from this container, so anything said in that call is not covered here. No later meeting notes mention CAP-72 (checked the docs index, `llms-full.txt`, and the Raven meeting-notes search).

## 1. What CAP-72 proposes

CAP-72 ("Contract signers for Stellar accounts", Draft, owner Dmytro Kozhevin, consulted Leigh McCulloch, Nicolas Barry, Anup Pani) adds a new `SignerKey` arm, `SIGNER_KEY_TYPE_SC_DELEGATED`, carrying an `SCAddress`. A G-account adds one with a normal `SetOptionsOp`. The signer can never sign a transaction (classic signature checks ignore it, and `extraSigners` rejects it with a new `txACCOUNT_DELEGATED_SIGNER_NOT_SUPPORTED`). It only counts inside Soroban authorization: when a G-account's auth entry carries delegates (CAP-71-01's `SorobanAddressCredentialsWithDelegates`), the host checks each delegate is on the `AccountEntry`, runs its `__check_auth` through `delegate_account_auth`, and adds its weight toward the account's Medium threshold (High if the context touches the account itself). The CAP also makes every G-account an implicit built-in contract, the "GAC", with `update_ed25519_signer`, `remove_ed25519_signer`, `update_delegated_signer`, `remove_delegated_signer`, `set_master_weight` and `update_thresholds`, all requiring High, so a contract signer can perform account recovery. The GAC is what lets Soroban change signers, which means Soroban now adjusts classic base reserves and releases sponsorships, and new signers added that way cannot be sponsored. The first draft ([6afb0f9](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/commit/6afb0f93cc5d0564d39c0bb070881b995a751c7b), Sep 16, 2025) kept the new signers in a contract data entry; three days later [7699c72](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/commit/7699c72ce286e725b7bca40dc4079a269b19a792) moved them into the `AccountEntry` because, in Dmytro's words in [PR #1789](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/pull/1789), the contract data approach "is more complex than I've originally anticipated, and just extending the `Signer` seems like a much easier way to go (as proven by the -100 lines of diffs in this PR)". Nothing has changed in the text since Oct 2025 beyond status housekeeping, and its Implementation and Test Cases sections still say TBD.

For the agent-wallet demo, the useful part is small: an x402 payment is a Soroban auth entry for a USDC `transfer`, so a policy contract added as a delegated signer can decide every agent payment while the wallet stays a plain G-account that any exchange can fund.

## 2. Objections and concerns actually raised

The thread had six participants. Three were supportive (Marcelo Salloum, Jake Urban, tupui), two raised design concerns (Leigh McCulloch, Dmytro himself), and one maintainer opposed the CAP outright (Tomer Weller). There are no comments between Sep 24, 2025 and Jan 26, 2026, none after Tomer's until Leigh's two replies on Oct 19, 2026, and the discussion is marked Closed. The "didn't get much support, and got postponed indefinitely" line is from Dmytro on Oct 19, 2026 in conversation (per the demo spec), not in the thread.

### Verified objections

**O1. Too much complexity for too little benefit; the real answer is contract accounts.** Tomer Weller (SDF), Jan 26, 2026, [comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-15610998): "I am .. not a fan of this CAP. This is adding a significant amount of cognitive overhead to Stellar's (already convoluted) wallets story while providing limited functionality. Contract accounts allow wallets to (1) support arbitrary signing schemes, (2) enforce arbitrary policies and (3) remove the need for base reserves, which in turn negates xlm lockups and opens up the ability for lazy instantiation of accounts and balances. The main use case supported by this cap is a small slither of (1): allowing passkeys to be a recovery signer for a classic account. This doesn't adhere to what is considered a passkey wallet in the industry as it still requires on-device key management. I feel the pain of limited ecosystem supports for contract accounts, and we're working on that, but this seems like a complex solution with limited benefits." This is the only outright opposition in the thread, and it is the last substantive comment before the CAP went quiet. Note what it attacks: the stated use case (passkey recovery), the wallet-story surface area (GAC, new signer type, new threshold rules), and the premise that G-accounts are worth extending at all.

**O2. The GAC interface leaks raw ed25519 bytes into tooling and user workflows.** Leigh McCulloch, Sep 24, 2025, [comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-14492869): "Some elements of the proposal do not interact well with strkeys ... `fn update_ed25519_signer(env: Env, key: BytesN<32>, weight: u32)` ... This will surface ed25519 in a form not as strkeys into developer tooling and user workflows. We should go to the effort of defining a new ScVal type, Signer, that supports the strkeys of signers." Dmytro pushed back the same day ([comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-14501669)): "`BytesN` is the standard format for the public keys in the smart contracts ... I don't think we should move the presentation layer logic into contracts." Leigh came back on Oct 19, 2026 ([comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-19053817)): "This will have subtle impacts on developer tooling too ... the stellar-cli manages keys, and protects them from leaked too, lifting them into fields by name and handling them safely. It can't do that for BytesN fields like it does for Address fields today." Unresolved.

**O3. The new signer type needs a new strkey, and a G-account as a delegated signer would display as something that doesn't look like a G-account.** Leigh, same Sep 24 comment: "Adding this signer type will require adding a new strkey for it, which unfortunately will represent the G-account in a form that doesn't look like a G-account ... I think if we limit delegation to contracts only would prevent the need to solve this problem. So the signer can only be a C." Dmytro: "Do we actually need a new strkey for this at all? ... I think we can consider this option ... I can see us dropping delegated G-signer support in case if we think it would never be necessary." He also floated collapsing the GAC interface to `update_signer(address, weight)/remove_signer(address)`, treating G as an ed25519 signer and C as a delegated signer. Leigh on Oct 19, 2026 ([comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-19053718)): displaying all signers as strkeys "happens in several APIs. One being Horizon". Unresolved, but both sides seem fine with contracts-only.

**O4. Soroban shouldn't be touching classic reserves and sponsorships.** Dmytro himself, Aug 14, 2025, [comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-14109288): "The only immediate issue that I see is that I don't think we want to mess with the classic semantics much (i.e. sponsorships, reserves etc.). I think we should be able to add the new signers on the Soroban side via a ContractData entry and not deal with the classic signers at all." He then wrote the CAP that way, reversed it in [PR #1789](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/pull/1789), and the CAP's own rationale now records the cost: "This sets a precedent for the classic base reserves being managed from the Soroban environment ... a G-account *must* hold a sufficient XLM balance in order to add new signers via its contract" and "There is also no full feature parity with the classic protocol, as using the sponsorships is not possible in Soroban." One thing has changed since: [CAP-73](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0073.md) (Final, Protocol 26) creates classic trustlines from Soroban with exactly the same reserve rules and no sponsorship, and its rationale says "Most of the design coincide with those for the G-account signer management in CAP-72". So the precedent Dmytro worried about now exists either way. The GAC is still extra surface, but the reserve-from-Soroban argument on its own is weaker than it was in 2025.

**O5. Delegated signers still can't do what people most want: sign transactions and classic operations.** tupui, Sep 22, 2025, [comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-14480593): a contract signer for transactions "would have been very handy ... make a full custodial solution with set of signers depending on rules like amount, time, recent volume, allow list ... exchanges, custodial solutions (like Fireblocks), etc. do not really support C addresses and it's going to take a long time until this happens (cf muxed account.)" Dmytro's reply ([comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-14480858)): transaction signing by contracts is "much more complicated than what's proposed here due to the risks of running VM for transaction validation 'for free'"; CAP-72 "is a much more realistic next step". Leigh's opening post gives the same reason. This is a scope limit, not an objection to the CAP, but it shapes expectations: CAP-72 can't make a G-account "fully managed by a contract".

**O6. Onboarding and reserve UX for the passkey use case is awkward.** Jake Urban, Jul 24, 2025, [comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-13882775): supportive of all four proposals ("Overall I'm very supportive"), but for Freighter "we still need to generate a seed phrase for the Stellar private key ... until the user funds the account with enough XLM to pay for the reserves and fees of adding a contract signer ... it would be possible for the user to delete the app and we'd have to generate a new account address". This is the same point Tomer makes harder: a passkey-on-G wallet still has a key to manage and reserves to fund.

**O7. Practical caveats from the one integrator who planned around it.** Nido's onboarding design, [nidohq/nido#165](https://github.com/nidohq/nido/issues/165) (Aug 7, 2026), has a "Forward compatibility: CAP-0072" section and lists: "exchanges still withdraw to classic G-addresses (G stays the landing zone); a one-time G-key signature is still needed to install the delegated signer; delegated signers cannot authorize classic operations (closing G later routes through the G-account contract to re-add a key, then merge, or G is left open); and per CAP-0072 the delegated signer's base reserve cannot be sponsored, so a persistent G is an ongoing per-user reserve cost rather than reclaimed at teardown." The last caveat is only true for signers added through the GAC; a `SetOptionsOp` signer can be sponsored today.

**O8. CAP-71-01 delegation has a known expressiveness gap that CAP-72 inherits.** Boyan Barakov (OpenZeppelin, brozorec), Sep 8, 2026, on the CAP-71 thread, [comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1784#discussioncomment-18349506): OZ's smart account did not adopt `delegate_account_auth` because "the host calls `D.__check_auth(P, S(D), C)` using the account's own `P` and `C`. The delegating account has no way to contribute to what `D` authenticates", which lets a submitter pick a weaker rule after signatures are collected in OZ's multi-rule design. He proposes a `delegate_account_auth_for_args` variant. CAP-72's G-account path is the same mechanism, so a delegated policy contract gets only the payload and the invocation contexts. For a single-rule policy like `agent_policy` that is enough; for OZ-style rule selection it isn't.

### Supportive comments, for the record

Marcelo Salloum (Jul 22, 2025, [comment](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763#discussioncomment-13851941)): "I absolutely love this idea ... existing businesses that wanted to support passkeys without having to walk away from G-accounts". Jake Urban: "very supportive". tupui: wants more, not less. The opening post got 5 upvotes; Dmytro's Aug 14 comment got 3; Tomer's got 1 thumbs up.

### Inferred reasons it stalled (inference)

* The only articulated use case was passkey recovery, and the person who runs SDF's wallet strategy said that use case is served better by contract accounts, which SDF was already investing in (Passkey Kit, Smart Account Kit; see the [2026-08-20 meeting notes](https://developers.stellar.org/meetings/2026/08/20#passkey-kit-vs-smart-account-kit)). Nobody in the thread argued that the use case was bigger than passkeys.
* The GAC roughly doubles the CAP (implicit contract per account, reserve accounting from the host, six account-management functions, a new High-threshold rule) and it is the part Leigh's tooling objections attach to. The core piece (one signer arm, counted in Soroban auth) is small, and CAP-71-01 already shipped the delegation plumbing in Protocol 27.
* Protocol bandwidth went elsewhere: CAP-73, CAP-77 and CAP-81 (Protocol 26), CAP-71 (27), CAP-83/85/86 (per the Jul 31, 2026 status commit). CAP-72 never got the Implementation section filled in, which is the prerequisite to moving past Draft.
* CAP-72 was the stated motivation for CAP-71 ([CAP-71-01 Motivation](https://github.com/stellar/stellar-protocol/blob/master/core/cap-0071-01.md)); once CAP-71 shipped on its own merits, CAP-72 lost its coupling to anything scheduled.

## 3. Design improvements, ranked

Ranked by how directly each answers a raised objection, weighted by cost. "Prototype now" means the demo can do it today in the host patch draft (`version-a/`), in `contracts/agent_policy/src/lib.rs`, or in the demo flow. "CAP revision" means only the spec and core can make it normative.

### 1. Split the CAP: ship the signer arm first, the GAC later (or never)

Answers O1 (complexity), O2 (the `BytesN` interface disappears with the GAC), O4 (no reserve or sponsorship changes from Soroban at all), O7 (a `SetOptionsOp` signer can be sponsored, so Nido's reserve caveat goes away).

What it is: CAP-72a keeps `SIGNER_KEY_TYPE_SC_DELEGATED`, `SetOptionsOp` support, the `extraSigners` rejection, and the host-side authentication algorithm. It drops the GAC, the implicit per-account contract, the `AccountEntry` updates from the host, and the "raise to High when a G-account call is in context" rule (there is no G-account call to be in context). The diff against today's text is mostly deletions. CAP-72b, if wanted later, adds the GAC on top.

Tradeoff: without the GAC a delegated signer cannot change signers or thresholds, so Leigh's original use case (passkey recovers a lost device key) is gone, and Nido's "set master weight to 0 and let the passkey control G" plan is gone too. That is the point: the use cases that remain (a policy co-signer on an account that still has a real key) are exactly the ones Tomer's objection doesn't reach, because the account keeps a key either way and the policy is the product. Recovery can come back as 72b once the signer type has proven itself.

Prototype now: yes, this is already the demo's shape. The spec's version A skips the GAC, `setup-b.sh`/`demo.mjs` attach the policy with `SetOptionsOp`, and the host patch only touches `check_account_authentication` and `get_signer_weight_from_account`. Make it explicit: the version-a README should say "implements CAP-72 without the GAC, which we propose as CAP-72a", so the patch is the reference implementation of the revised scope rather than a shortcut.

### 2. Delegated signers never count toward High, and never alone satisfy a threshold that changes the account

Answers O1's "buggy or malicious contract takes over the account" reading, and the general audit worry in the CAP's own Security Concerns ("every contract signer added to the account has to be a trusted and audited contract").

What it is: a normative rule that delegated weight is only added when the authorization context contains no account-management call (no GAC call in 72a; in 72b, any GAC function). Equivalent: delegated weight counts toward Medium only. A delegate can then authorize transfers and contract calls, but can never add a signer, change thresholds, or lower the master weight, no matter how it is weighted. The owner's ed25519 keys keep sole control of the account's configuration.

Tradeoff: kills "passkey-only" G-accounts (which Tomer already said are not real passkey wallets) and makes the GAC in 72b usable only by ed25519 signers, which raises the question of why 72b exists. It also slightly weakens CAP-72 for a multisig-style delegate (a G-account "treasury policy" that should be able to rotate signers), but that case can be a later relaxation with an explicit per-signer flag.

Prototype now: yes. The demo already configures master weight 2, delegate weight 1, thresholds 1/1/2, so the policy alone can't reach High. In the host patch, make it a rule rather than a configuration: in the CAP-72 branch of `check_account_authentication`, add delegate weights only when no `Context` in the auth tree targets a G-address (in 72a there is no such call, so the check is a cheap guard that documents intent), and add a test that a delegated signer with weight 255 still fails a High-threshold check. The demo runner should print the rule next to the signer table ("delegated signers can't change this account's signers or thresholds").

### 3. Contracts only: the delegated signer is an `SCAddress` of type contract (or just a 32-byte contract id)

Answers O3 (no new strkey; a contract signer displays as the C strkey it already has, in Horizon and everywhere else), part of O2, and O8 indirectly (a G-account as delegate adds nothing a C can't do, and the G-as-delegate case is where CAP-71-01's "same payload, same context" gets confusing).

What it is: `SetOptionsOp` rejects `SC_ADDRESS_TYPE_ACCOUNT` delegates (or the arm becomes `Hash delegatedContract`, which is smaller and can never hold an account). Leigh proposed it, Dmytro said "we can consider this option".

Tradeoff: no G-account-as-cosigner-of-G-account through Soroban. That is already possible with plain ed25519 signers, so nothing is lost for the agent case. Picking `Hash` over `SCAddress` closes the door on future address types (muxed contracts from CAP-84, say) without another XDR change; `SCAddress` restricted by validation keeps the door open at the cost of one more rejection path. Either is fine; `SCAddress` with validation is the smaller change to today's text.

Prototype now: yes. The host patch should reject non-contract delegates on the `AccountEntry` lookup, and the runner should refuse to build a `SetOptionsOp` with a G-address delegate. The demo's signer table should show the policy as its plain C strkey, which demonstrates the "no new strkey" claim.

### 4. Keep a new `SignerKey` arm rather than reusing an existing one

This is a candidate the demo asked to evaluate, and the answer is no. The three existing non-ed25519 arms all have classic meaning: `PRE_AUTH_TX` is satisfied by a transaction hash match, `HASH_X` by revealing a preimage, `ED25519_SIGNED_PAYLOAD` by an ed25519 signature over a payload. Smuggling a contract id into any of them means classic signature verification would treat it as that kind of signer, so the "ignored during transaction signature verification" property has to be special-cased on content, which is worse for every decoder than a new arm. Every ecosystem decoder (Horizon, RPC, SDKs, CLI, Lab, explorers) already has to learn the new arm's bytes to show an account correctly, and that cost is the same whichever encoding is chosen; a new arm makes the failure mode an explicit decode error rather than a silently misread signer. The ecosystem cost is real (it is why the demo can't use the stock CLI or Lab in version A), but it is one enum value plus one 32-byte payload, the same size of change as CAP-71's new credential type, which shipped. CAP revision only; the prototype already uses the new arm.

### 5. Per-account cap on delegated signers

Low value. The CAP already bounds total signers at `MAX_SIGNERS` (20) and signatures plus delegates at 20 per auth check, and each delegate's `__check_auth` is metered Soroban execution paid by the transaction, so there is no free-execution problem (that is why contract signers for classic transactions are out of scope, O5). A cap of, say, 4 delegated signers would shrink the worst-case auth-time work and make the "which contract controls this account" question easier to audit, at the cost of an arbitrary number in the protocol. Suggest a non-normative recommendation instead: wallets should show delegated signers prominently. Prototype: not worth code; the demo has one.

### 6. On-ledger per-delegate scope (delegate may only authorize calls to contract X)

Answers the "buggy delegate" worry with a protocol-enforced allowlist checked before the contract runs. Tradeoff: more XDR in `SignerKey` (an optional contract address or list), `SetOptionsOp` validation changes, and it duplicates what the policy contract already does, with weaker expressiveness (no amounts, no destinations). It also changes the `SignerKey` shape from "identity" to "identity plus policy", which every decoder and every wallet UI would need to render. Only worth it if reviewers want a non-Turing-complete guarantee; otherwise the policy contract is the scope. CAP revision only. The demo already shows the contract-side version: `agent_policy` pins `rules.token` and rejects anything that isn't `transfer(wallet, allowed_seller, amount <= max)`, with typed errors. Keep that and point at it.

### 7. Require ed25519 weight to stay at or above High

A stronger form of improvement 2, enforced at `SetOptionsOp` apply time: after applying the op, the sum of non-delegated signer weights (master plus ed25519 signers) must be at least the High threshold, otherwise `SET_OPTIONS_THRESHOLD_OUT_OF_RANGE` or a new result code. It guarantees no account can ever be left controllable only by contracts. Tradeoff: it forbids the passkey-only and Nido-style accounts outright, and it is a classic-side invariant with awkward interactions with existing accounts that already have master weight 0 and only pre-auth or hash-x signers (those would have to be exempt when no delegated signer exists). Improvement 2 gets most of the safety with none of the classic-side complexity, so rank this below it and offer it in the thread as an option rather than a recommendation. Prototype now: the runner can enforce it client-side (refuse to build a `SetOptionsOp` that would leave ed25519 weight below High) and print the invariant; the core-side check is CAP-only.

### 8. Fix the delegation expressiveness gap (CAP-71 follow-up, not CAP-72)

O8 is about CAP-71-01. If OZ's `delegate_account_auth_for_args` lands, G-account delegation picks it up for free. For CAP-72 the useful note is that the built-in G-account `__check_auth` has no signature payload of its own to carry extra arguments, so any per-delegate parameter would have to live in the delegate's signature `SCVal`, which `agent_policy` already controls (`AgentSignature`). Nothing to change in the demo.

### 9. Demo-flow additions that preempt objections

* Sponsor the delegated signer's reserve from the funder with `BeginSponsoringFutureReserves` around the `SetOptionsOp`, and print the account's reserve before and after. That shows O4/O7 don't apply to the `SetOptionsOp` path.
* Print the fee of a delegated payment next to a plain G-account payment, so the "cost of policy" is a number in the thread.
* Keep the failing direct submission in the run of show; it is the one piece of evidence that the network, not the facilitator, enforced the rule.
* State plainly which tools can't decode the new arm yet (CLI, Lab, Horizon) and what the patch for each is, since ecosystem decode cost is the honest remaining objection to improvement 4.

## 4. Pitch for the discussion thread

Something Steph or Tyler could paste into [#1763](https://github.com/orgs/stellar/discussions/1763), adjusted to taste.

> We'd like to reopen CAP-72 with a different use case and a smaller scope.
>
> The use case is agent wallets. An AI agent paying for x402 APIs needs a wallet whose spending rules (per-payment cap, daily budget, seller allowlist) are enforced by the network, not by the agent's own code, because the agent can be buggy or prompt-injected. A smart account does that today. But the agent's owner funds it from an exchange, a ramp or a treasury, and those send classic `Payment` operations to G-addresses. CAP-73 lets contracts create trustlines now, and it still doesn't let an exchange pay a C-address. So the agent wallet has to be a G-account, and the policy has to be a contract. Every x402 payment on Stellar is a Soroban auth entry for a USDC `transfer`, which is the one thing a CAP-72 delegated signer can sign. We built it: a G-account funded by a plain payment, a policy contract attached as a delegated signer with `SetOptionsOp`, an agent key that isn't a signer on anything, and a $2 payment that the network rejects in `__check_auth` while the two $1 payments go through. [link to the demo]
>
> We also think the objections in this thread point at a narrower CAP, and we'd propose it as CAP-72a:
>
> 1. No G-account contract. Delegated signers are added, changed and removed only with `SetOptionsOp`. That removes the implicit per-account contract, the reserve and sponsorship accounting from the host, and the `BytesN` interface Leigh raised. A `SetOptionsOp` signer can be sponsored like any other, so the "ongoing unsponsored reserve" caveat goes away too. Recovery flows can come later as 72b if the signer type earns it.
> 2. Delegated weight counts toward Medium only, never High. A delegated contract can authorize transfers and contract calls, but can never add a signer, change thresholds or lower the master weight, whatever its weight. The owner's keys keep sole control of the account's configuration, so a buggy or compromised policy contract can lose at most what the policy allows.
> 3. Contracts only. A delegated signer is a contract address, so it displays as the C strkey it already has, in Horizon and everywhere else, and no new strkey is needed.
>
> What's left is one `SignerKey` arm, `SetOptionsOp` accepting it, classic signature checks ignoring it, and the host counting it in Soroban auth through the CAP-71-01 plumbing that already shipped in Protocol 27. Our host patch for the demo is that change, with tests, and we'd be glad to turn it into the Implementation section.
>
> On Tomer's point: agreed that passkey recovery on a G-account is a thin slice of what contract accounts offer, and agreed that contract accounts are the long-term answer. The agent case is different in kind. The account keeps a real key, the policy is the whole product, and the thing blocking it is not wallet UX but that the money arrives by classic payment. We don't think this competes with smart accounts; it gives the people who can only fund G-addresses today a way to put a Soroban policy in front of the money.
